# Loot Drop Systems: Minecraft Dungeons (2020) vs. Minecraft Dungeons II (2026)
*Design reference for Abysmal (2.5D first-person dungeon crawler roguelike). Researched Oct 7, 2026.*

## MINECRAFT DUNGEONS (2020)

**1. Rarity tiers.** Three base rarities plus one special: Common (white) → Rare (turquoise/green) → Unique (orange), plus Gilded (a gold-bordered modifier that can apply to standard or unique items). Rarity mostly affects salvage value (emeralds) and enchantment economics; Rare items have slightly better base stats and a +1 higher power cap than Common. Sources: RPS unique guide (rockpapershotgun.com), gameslearningsociety.org wiki FAQ

**2. Drop rates / what affects them.** Difficulty is the main lever: higher difficulties (Default → Adventure → Apocalypse) expand the mission loot pool — some gear only becomes obtainable above Default — and community consensus is that harder difficulties improve rare-drop odds. Luck-enhancing enchantments (e.g., the Looting enchantment) further improve odds. One early guide claimed mission level doesn't change rarity likelihood, so the documented levers are difficulty tier + luck effects, not character level. Sources: vintageisthenewold.com FAQ, gameslearningsociety.org wiki

**3. Loot table structure.** Drops are drawn from per-mission loot pools — each mission has a defined set of weapons/armor/artifacts it can drop, which is why farming guides point at specific levels. Gear drops scale in power level with the difficulty played; the Blacksmith NPC can raise an item's power level after campaign milestones. Artifacts (active abilities) drop as their own category and are never enchantable. Sources: gameranx.com farming guide, gamivo.com guide

**4. Uniques.** Uniques are named items with a built-in unique ability/effect, drawn from mission pools and boss drops. Their rolled enchantments are random but can be rerolled at the camp Enchanter for gold. You cannot upgrade a Rare into a Unique — it must drop (or be bought from the camp merchant). Sources: gameslearningsociety.org wiki, RPS

**5. Enchantment system.** Each weapon/armor piece has up to 3 enchantment slots with randomly offered enchantment choices. You earn 1 enchantment point per character level-up; maxing one enchantment costs 6 points (1+2+3), so 18 points fully enchants an item. On Adventure+ difficulty, "Powerful" enchantment variants cost 8 points to max (24 per item). Salvaging an item refunds all invested points, so points are a circulating currency, not a sunk cost — the game explicitly wants you to enchant freely. Late-game additions: the Enchantsmith can replace unwanted enchantment choices for Gold (earned in Ancient Hunts), and Gilded gear (from Ancient Hunts up to power 251, or the Piglin Merchant) comes with a bonus pre-filled enchantment slot. Source: GameSpot enchanting guide (gamespot.com), gamivo

**6. Pity / guaranteed drops.** No documented pity timer found. The closest things to guarantees: boss farming (the cow-level boss is community-reported at roughly 50% unique per kill) and merchants selling random inventory including uniques. Source: gameranx

## MINECRAFT DUNGEONS II (2026)

**1. Rarity tiers.** Four rarities now: Common → Rare → Special (new) → Unique. 116 uniques total: 29 melee, 11 ranged, 76 armor pieces forming 19 complete armor sets. No unique artifacts or talismans exist. Key mechanical differences per rarity (from community game-data mining): each rarity has an item-power offset (Common/Rare drop at −2 power, Special at −1, Unique at 0 — so uniques always drop at full strength for your level) and a count of re-rollable bonus effects (Common 0, Rare 1, Special 2, Unique 1 plus one fixed signature effect). Source: metabot.gg uniques guide

**2. Drop rates / what affects them.** Every drop rolls rarity from a weighted table, and the source modifies the table — this is the big structural change. Regular enemy drops: 50/25/20/5 (C/R/S/U). Bosses: no commons, weights Rare 30 / Special 20 / Unique 10 → 50% Rare, 33.3% Special, 16.7% Unique. Mini-bosses: no commons → 50/40/10. The Soul Storm reward chest is the best farm in the game: no commons and Rare weight crushed to ~1% → ~19.8% Unique, ~79.2% Special. Fancy chests and piggy-bank mobs: no commons → 10% Unique. Wooden chests halve commons → ~6.7% Unique. Two gating rules: on Easy difficulty, Special and Unique weights are multiplied to zero (uniques literally cannot drop), and drop quality improves slightly with hero level. Merchants are excluded from selling uniques entirely. Source: metabot.gg uniques guide

**3. Loot table structure.** Same philosophy as D1 — area/region-based loot pools (a unique drops wherever its base item can drop) — but with a reworked gear model: 12 slots (4 separate armor pieces — helmet, chestplate, leggings, boots — plus melee, ranged, 3 artifacts, 3 talismans). Talismans are a new passive-bonus category, and one of them, the Lucky Clover, doubles the chance of additional loot drops — luck as an equippable build choice. Power rating is the average of equipped gear. Sources: allthings.how trailer breakdown, metabot.gg, minecraftdungeonsiiwiki.wiki

**4. Uniques.** Elegant redesign: a Unique is not a separate loot entry — it's a base item that rolled Unique rarity and transformed into its named version (Sword→The Burning Blade, Greatbow→Humbler Heartstring, Bounty Hunter Helmet→Dauntless Horns). Each carries one fixed signature effect plus one re-rollable effect. Several uniques carry a Unique tier of a regular enchantment — a stronger version of a standard enchant. Source: metabot.gg uniques guide

**5. Enchantment system — completely overhauled.** Enchantments are no longer baked into items. You find enchantment books in the world, they go into a personal library, and the Enchantsmith NPC applies them to any gear slot and lets you move them later — enchantments level up over time instead of dying with the item. You still earn enchantment points from leveling (cap reported at 99). The Blacksmith spends Echo Shards (new crafting currency from clearing dungeons and killing Soul Corrupted enemies) to upgrade any item to your current power level and to reroll its random bonus effect. Sources: tech-insider.org launch guide, beebom.com Enchantsmith guide, player2.net.au review

**6. Pity / guaranteed drops.** Two confirmed systems: repeatable boss fights grant a guaranteed unique on first victory (subsequent kills use standard RNG), and some bosses have fixed guaranteed drops (Gauntlets from the Prime Enchanter, Wonderful Wheat from the Mosstrosity). No pity timer documented. Sources: gamingally.net unique gear, metabot.gg bosses guide

**7. What's new vs. D1.** (a) The Sift — a new dimension manifesting as Sift portals leading to procedurally generated optional dungeons in three variants (Thrive/Endure/Flow) with tougher Soul-like enemies that drop emeralds and Echo Shards. (b) Special rarity tier inserted between Rare and Unique. (c) Unique-as-rarity-roll instead of unique-as-separate-item. (d) Enchantment library + Enchantsmith/Blacksmith decoupling build investment from item churn, funded by Echo Shards. (e) Talismans as a third passive layer, including loot-affecting options. (f) 12 gear slots vs. 6, with mix-and-match armor pieces instead of full sets. (g) Ranged ammo replaced by cooldown refill. Sources: theaureview.com, techfeatured.com, allthings.how

## Gaps and honesty notes
- D2 numbers come primarily from metabot.gg's community game-data mining (published ~2 days ago). Best available source this early, but single-source and datamined — treat exact percentages as "reported," not gospel.
- D1 never had officially published drop rates; its section is built from guides and community consensus, which occasionally conflict.
- No pity timer confirmed in either game. First-kill boss guarantees (D2) are the closest mechanic.
- The Sift's exact loot-table relationship to the overworld isn't documented yet — only that Sifts yield more emeralds and Echo Shards.

## Design takeaways (worth stealing for the roguelike)
1. Unique-as-rarity-roll (D2) is the single best idea here: one loot pool per item family, and the rarity roll transforms the item into its named version. Collapses two systems into one and makes every drop exciting.
2. Rarity power offsets — letting top rarity always drop at full strength for your level makes rarity feel structural, not cosmetic. Pairs beautifully with per-death power scaling.
3. Source-modified rarity tables (boss 16.7% vs. trash 5% unique) is transparent, farmable design — players can feel the odds change by choosing harder content. Consider exposing these numbers in-game; D2's community had to datamine them.
4. Enchantment library decoupling (D2) solves the roguelike's core tension: if items are disposable per run, permanent build investment has to live outside items. A persistent enchantment library + a reroll/upgrade sink gives long-term progression without invalidating run-based loot.
5. Blacksmith power catch-up — upgrading a beloved item to current power for a currency cost is anti-obsolescence design; it respects player attachment, which matters in a game about exponential stat scaling.
6. First-kill guaranteed unique — a perfect onboarding hook: the player's first boss always pays off, teaching them what uniques are.
7. Luck as equipment (Lucky Clover) rather than a hidden stat — makes "luck builds" a visible, choosable playstyle. (Note: this project already merged loot axes into a single Luck skill — a Luck talisman/gear layer would stack naturally with that.)
8. No uniques from merchants — protecting the chase. If the roguelike has shops, keep the top tier drop-only.
9. Easy difficulty zeroes top rarities — difficulty-gated aspiration gives players a reason to push tiers beyond just bigger numbers.

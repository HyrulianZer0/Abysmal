================================================================
ABYSMAL — Apex Bosses: Lore & Strategies
Producer/Director: Aethelwarden Solas
================================================================

The 16 named apex bosses of the roster. Each section gives lore drawn
fresh from the figure's public-domain folklore roots (no borrowed game
lore, stats, or designs), its attack patterns, and how to beat it.

At the end: a PROPOSED boss -> body-slot mapping for the 16-slot
signature-drop system (one boss per slot). PROPOSAL ONLY — James
decides. Slots: head, face, neck, shoulders, chest, back (cape/cloak),
arms, hands, waist, legs, feet, ring (left), ring (right), trinket,
weapon, off-hand.

================================================================
1. CERBERUS — Greek, base: Hellhound
================================================================
LORE: The hound of Hades, guardian of the underworld's gates. Three
heads, a serpent for a tail, a mane of snakes. He lets every soul in
and none out. Heracles, as one of his labours, was permitted to wrestle
him bare-handed — and did.
ATTACKS:
- Triple Bite: the three heads strike in sequence, front arc.
- Tail Serpent: the tail lashes sideways with little warning.
- Gate Roar: a bellow that staggers anything in front of him.
COUNTERS: Fight from one flank so only one head tracks you. The roar
has a long windup — step out of the front arc. Punish after the triple
bite; the heads need a breath before the next set.

================================================================
2. MEDUSA — Greek, base: Gorgon
================================================================
LORE: Once a beautiful priestess, cursed so her hair became serpents
and her gaze turned living flesh to stone. Perseus slew her using a
mirrored shield so he never had to meet her eyes.
ATTACKS:
- Petrifying Gaze: telegraphed eye-glow; anyone facing her when it
  fires is slowed, then stone-held.
- Serpent Hair: the snakes strike independently, multi-hit, close
  range.
- Stone Shards: chips of old victims thrown as projectiles.
COUNTERS: Turn or strafe during the gaze windup — never face it.
Attack hard during the long gaze cooldown. The snakes are separate
targets; clearing them buys safe windows.

================================================================
3. TYPHON — Greek, base: Ur-Dragon class
================================================================
LORE: The storm-giant the gods themselves fled — a hundred serpent
heads, a voice of storms, father of monsters with Echidna. Zeus
defeated him and buried him under Mount Etna; the volcano's rumbling
is Typhon breathing below.
ATTACKS:
- Storm Winds: arena-wide gusts that knock you back.
- Serpent Volley: the lesser heads spit projectiles in fans.
- Eruption: ground bursts under the player, telegraphed by cracks.
COUNTERS: Keep moving — the winds punish standing still. Step off
cracked ground before eruption. The main head is the damage target;
the lesser heads are a distraction to dodge, not to farm.

================================================================
4. ECHIDNA — Greek, base: Lamia class
================================================================
LORE: "Mother of Monsters" — half woman, half serpent, dwelling in a
cave, mate of Typhon and mother of Cerberus, the Hydra, the Chimera
and more. She does not hunt alone; her children do it for her.
ATTACKS:
- Brood Call: summons lesser monsters in waves.
- Tail Sweep: a wide ground sweep around her coils.
- Venom Spit: arcing poison pools.
COUNTERS: Decide fast: burn her down and eat the swarm, or clear the
brood first for a clean duel. Never stand in the sweep ring — fight
from range or dive past the tail. Step out of venom pools at once.

================================================================
5. FENRIR — Norse, base: Great Wolf
================================================================
LORE: The monstrous wolf, son of Loki, who grew too great for the gods
to leave loose. They bound him with Gleipnir, a chain forged from
impossible things — a cat's footfall, a woman's beard, a fish's breath.
Tyr lost a hand to the binding. At the end of the world, Fenrir breaks
free and devours Odin.
ATTACKS:
- Bone Bite: a single crushing frontal bite.
- Pounce: a long telegraphed leap, lands where you stood.
- Death Howl: a fear stagger at close range.
COUNTERS: Never trade the bite — it is a frontal cone, sidestep it.
The pounce always lands where you were standing; keep moving and it
misses by itself. Punish the long recovery after a missed pounce.

================================================================
6. JÖRMUNGANDR — Norse, base: World Wurm
================================================================
LORE: The world serpent, cast into the sea by Odin, where he grew
until he encircled the whole world, tail in mouth. At the end of the
world he rises to fight Thor, who kills him — and dies nine steps
later from his venom.
ATTACKS:
- Tail Sweep: arena-edge sweeps; the rim of the room is dangerous.
- Venom Spray: poison pools that linger.
- Coil Surge: a rising wave that pushes the player back.
COUNTERS: Poison resistance or a cleanse is near-mandatory. Fight
near the head, not the rim — the tail owns the edges. Clear venom
pools by moving, never by standing in them.

================================================================
7. SURTR — Norse, base: Fire Giant
================================================================
LORE: Lord of Muspelheim, the world of fire, keeper of a sword
brighter than the sun. At the end of the world he marches south and
his flames consume gods and earth alike.
ATTACKS:
- Flame Sword Arc: huge sweeping arcs, front and sides.
- Fire Wave: a travelling wall of flame.
- Eruption Pillars: fire columns under the player, telegraphed.
COUNTERS: Fire resistance first. The sword arc has a slow windup and
a long recovery — that recovery is your window. Never stand on
scorched ground. Ranged attacks shine during fire-wave windup.

================================================================
8. FAFNIR — Norse, base: Elder Wyrm
================================================================
LORE: A dwarf, son of Hreidmar, who murdered his own father for cursed
gold and became a dragon to guard it, sleeping on the hoard. Sigurd
slew him by digging a pit in his path and striking from below, then
tasted the dragon's blood and understood the speech of birds.
ATTACKS:
- Poison Breath: a frontal cone of lingering venom cloud.
- Tail Sweep: a heavy rear arc.
- Hoard Charge: a straight-line rush across the room.
COUNTERS: Poison resistance. Stay to the flank — the breath is
frontal, the tail is rear. Sidestep the charge; it cannot turn, and
the recovery after it is the best damage window in the fight.

================================================================
9. BABA YAGA — Slavic, base: Witch
================================================================
LORE: The witch of the deep forest, living in a hut that stands on
chicken legs and turns to face whoever approaches, behind a fence
topped with skulls. She flies in a mortar, steering with a pestle,
sweeping her trail away with a broom. Heroes who answer her questions
right may leave with a gift; the rude become dinner.
ATTACKS:
- Mortar Dive: a flying slam, telegraphed by its shadow.
- Pestle Sweep: wide melee arcs.
- Skull Volley: fence skulls fire projectiles.
- Hex: a casting debuff that slows and weakens.
COUNTERS: Watch the shadow, not the witch — the dive lands on the
shadow. Interrupt the hex cast. Destroy skull totems to thin the
volley. She pauses after a dive; that is the opening.

================================================================
10. KOSCHEI THE DEATHLESS — Slavic, base: Lich
================================================================
LORE: Koschei cannot be killed because his death is not in him. It is
hidden: a needle, inside an egg, inside a duck, inside a hare, inside
an iron chest, buried or locked away far from his body. Find the
needle and break it, and the Deathless dies like anyone else.
ATTACKS:
- Death Bolts: heavy ranged magic.
- Deathless Guard: invulnerable phases while a hidden vessel lives.
- Bone Summons: skeletal adds that screen him.
- Life Drain: a channeled beam that heals him.
COUNTERS: Each phase, find and break the hidden vessel — that ends
the invulnerable phase. Interrupt the drain channel at all costs.
Ignore the summons when a vessel is exposed; they are the distraction.

================================================================
11. HUMBABA — Mesopotamian, base: Cedar Guardian
================================================================
LORE: Guardian of the Cedar Forest, appointed by the god Enlil, from
the oldest written story we have — Gilgamesh, carved some four
thousand years ago. His face is terror, his voice is a flood, his
breath is fire. Gilgamesh and Enkidu slew him and took the cedars.
ATTACKS:
- Cedar Slam: trunk-sized arms smash the ground, shockwave ring.
- Terror Roar: a fear stagger in a wide radius.
- Root Snare: vines erupt under the player, telegraphed by cracks.
- Boulder Throw: slow, heavy, arena-wide AoE.
COUNTERS: Step off cracked ground before the snare erupts. The slam
recovery is long — that is the window. Spread out from the roar's
center. Boulders are slow; one sidestep clears them.

================================================================
12. SHUTEN-DŌJI — Japanese, base: Oni
================================================================
LORE: The drunken demon king of Mount Ōe, who raided the capital and
carried off maidens to his mountain palace. Minamoto no Raikō and his
retainers defeated him the clever way: they got him drunk on poisoned
sake, waited for him to sleep, and struck while he dreamed.
ATTACKS:
- Great Club Slam: slow, devastating overhead blows.
- Sake Breath: a drunken haze that confuses and slows.
- Oni Charge: a bull rush across the room.
- Drunken Frenzy: he speeds up as the fight goes on.
COUNTERS: Never trade the club — dodge the windup, always. He pauses
to drink mid-fight; that pause is the legendary opening, take it.
Confusion protection or distance beats the sake breath. End it fast:
frenzy means the fight gets harder the longer it lasts.

================================================================
13. YAMATA NO OROCHI — Japanese, base: Hydra
================================================================
LORE: The eight-headed, eight-tailed serpent that demanded a maiden a
year from a grieving family. Susanoo, god of storms, set out eight vats
of sake, waited for all eight heads to drink and sleep, and cut the
serpent down — finding the great sword Kusanagi in its tail.
ATTACKS:
- Eight-Head Volley: bites from many angles at once.
- Drunken Stagger: after drinking, the heads droop and slow.
- Tail Sweep: a massive rear arc from eight tails.
- Storm Breath: a wide elemental cone.
COUNTERS: Focus one head at a time — each head downed thins the
volley. When the heads droop after drinking, that is the damage
window. Never stand in the tail arc; the front is safer than the rear.

================================================================
14. RAVANA — Hindu epic, base: Rakshasa
================================================================
LORE: The ten-headed demon king of Lanka from the Ramayana, scholar,
musician, and warrior, who abducted Sita and would not yield her.
Rama, aided by Hanuman's army, besieged Lanka and slew him after a
duel that shook the island.
ATTACKS:
- Ten-Head Volley: multi-projectile fans from many heads.
- Sorcery Bolts: heavy aimed magic.
- Blade Flurry: a fast melee storm at close range.
- Illusion Doubles: phantom Ravanas that mirror his attacks.
COUNTERS: The true Ravana's illusions flicker — learn the tell and
ignore the fakes. Dodge volleys laterally, never backward. The blade
flurry has a long recovery; that is the punish window.

================================================================
15. ANZU — Mesopotamian, base: Anzu
================================================================
LORE: The great storm-bird, lion-headed eagle, who stole the Tablet of
Destinies from Enlil and hid it on a mountain. The god Ninurta hunted
him down and took it back. The storm itself was his accomplice.
ATTACKS:
- Storm Dive: a plunging aerial slam, telegraphed by shadow.
- Wind Gusts: knockback walls across the room.
- Lightning Strikes: marked ground zones that detonate.
- Tablet Pulse: a radial shockwave.
COUNTERS: Move out of marked zones before lightning lands. The dive
recovery is long — punish it. Position with your back to a wall so
gusts cannot throw you into hazards.

================================================================
16. THE TARASQUE — French, base: Tarasque
================================================================
LORE: The river-dragon of the Rhône at Tarascon: lion's head, ox's
body, tortoise shell, six legs, a serpent's tail. Knights could not
kill it; in the old telling it was Saint Martha who tamed it — not
with a sword, but with prayer and a ribbon.
ATTACKS:
- Shell Charge: a rolling charge, fast in a straight line.
- Tail Lash: a heavy rear arc.
- River Surge: a water wave across the room.
- Bite: a short-range snap.
COUNTERS: Sidestep the charge — it cannot turn, and the recovery is
long. The shell shrugs off frontal hits; attack the softer underside
after a missed charge. Water resistance helps against the surge.

PROPOSED BOSS -> BODY-SLOT MAPPING (James decides)
================================================================
One boss per slot. Each boss's signature drop is a weapon or armor
piece for its slot — 16 bosses, one full set to chase.

- Head        — Cerberus       (three-headed guardian helm)
- Face        — Medusa         (petrifying visage mask)
- Neck        — Echidna        (serpent-coil torc)
- Shoulders   — Fafnir         (dragon-scale pauldrons)
- Chest       — Typhon         (storm-giant stormplate)
- Back        — The Tarasque   (river-dragon shell cape)
- Arms        — Humbaba        (cedar-bark vambraces)
- Hands       — Fenrir         (wolf-claw gauntlets)
- Waist       — Jörmungandr    (world-serpent belt)
- Legs        — Yamata no Orochi (eight-tail scale greaves)
- Feet        — Anzu           (storm-bird talon boots)
- Ring (left) — Koschei        (death-needle ring)
- Ring (right)— Ravana         (ten-headed signet)
- Trinket     — Baba Yaga      (chicken-legged hut charm)
- Weapon      — Surtr          (world-fire sword)
- Off-hand    — Shuten-dōji    (demon king's sake gourd)

Notes on the mapping: Surtr's sword and Orochi's tail-sword (Kusanagi)
both compete for Weapon — Surtr takes it, Orochi's greaves carry the
serpent's scale instead; Kusanagi can still appear in Orochi's lore.
Koschei's ring and Ravana's signet give both ring slots a story.

# Lore research report — Abysmal thin-lore creatures

**Date:** 2026-11-08 (research only — nothing invented, no lore entries written, no files or code changed; director decides)

**Coverage:** 56 creatures. **Recommendations: 4 CLEAR, 48 KEEP, 4 ADD.**

**Method:** Broader sources searched directly for 40 creatures (mythology, folklore, bestiaries, wikis, public-domain texts). The other 16 were assessed from the lore doc's own entries in `design-notes/bestiary-lore/part-1..7.md`, read fresh this pass. Every doc claim checked held up — the doc's assessments were accurate on all 40 searched, so its text is trusted where not re-searched. Each entry below gives a short sourced snippet and a recommendation.

**Count note:** the 53 part-listed flags plus the 3 clear-delta candidates (Pale Horse, Strigoi, Afanc — not in the part lists) = 56 unique creatures. The 4 proposed adds overlap with the 53 (they're already called thin in the lore prose); the ADD recommendation is about the behavior-spec flag.

## CLEAR — 4 (real tradition supports the creature)

### Pale Horse — CLEAR
Revelation 6:7–8: "I looked, and there before me was a pale horse! Its rider was named Death, and Hades was following close behind him" — Greek *chlōrós*, pale green; given power over a fourth of the earth. A named figure in a canonical text with its own story, not a game label. *Sources: Revelation 6:7–8 (BibleGateway); Wikipedia, "Four Horsemen of the Apocalypse."*

### Strigoi — CLEAR
Romanian folklore, substantial and well documented: *strigoi vii* (living witch/sorcerer) vs *strigoi mort* (dead vampire); documented by Tudor Pamfile (*Mitologie românească*), Cantemir, Burada (1882), Murgoci (1926); word from Latin *strix/striga* (owl/witch). A real named creature with a real tradition. *Sources: Wikipedia, "Strigoi"; Pamfile, Cantemir, Burada, Murgoci as cited there.*

### Afanc — CLEAR
Welsh lake monster with multiple tales: Peredur fab Efrawg (*Mabinogion*), Hu Gadarn and the *Ychen Bannog* oxen dragging it from Llyn Llion (flood myth), the maiden-and-chains tale, Lewys Glyn Cothi (15th c.), and Llyn yr Afanc named after it. A real named creature with a story cycle. *Sources: Wikipedia, "Afanc"; Mabinogion; Lewys Glyn Cothi.*

### Falcon Sphinx — CLEAR (new recommendation, beyond the proposed 3)
The hieracosphinx is a real, attested Egyptian form: a falcon-headed sphinx linked to Horus. A sandstone example sits in the British Museum, from Ramesses II's temple at Abu Simbel (c. 1250 BCE); a hieracosphinx ivory panel from Nimrud (ca. 800–600 BCE) is in the Penn Museum. The Greek-derived terms androsphinx / criosphinx / hieracosphinx distinguish the forms. "Falcon Sphinx" is the plain-English rendering of a real attested type. *Sources: British Museum via Ancient Egypt Blog; Penn Museum, "An Ivory Sphinx from Nimrud"; Mythopouch, "Ancient Egyptian Sphinxes."*

## ADD — 4 (no standalone traditional figure under that exact name)

These 4 are already called thin in the lore prose; the ADD is the behavior-spec flag.

### Possessed Cultist — ADD
Demonic possession is a real, deep tradition — Mesopotamian incantations, Gospel exorcisms, the Catholic *Rituale Romanum* (1614, revised 1999), the Jewish dybbuk, Islamic *ruqyah*/jinn, Sumerian origins. But no standalone traditional figure is called a "Possessed Cultist": possession is a condition, and "cultist" is a modern pulp word. The background is real; the creature name is not. *Sources: The Mystica, "Exorcism"; American Myths, Legends, and Tall Tales encyclopedia; Book of Tobit (Asmodeus); Josephus (Eleazar).*

### Contract Devil — ADD
The Faust legend is rich — 1587 chapbook *Historia von D. Johann Fausten*, Marlowe's *Doctor Faustus* (c. 1592), Goethe's *Faust*, Stith Thompson Motif-Index M210 "Bargain with the devil" / M211 "Man sells soul to devil," Theophilus of Adana as predecessor. But no standalone traditional creature is called a "Contract Devil": the devil who bargains is a role, and Mephistopheles is the named figure. The flag is about the name, not the background. *Sources: Wikipedia, "Faust" / "Mephistopheles"; Stith Thompson Motif-Index.*

### Ashen Titan — ADD
The Titans are real and deep (Hesiod's *Theogony*: the twelve children of Uranus and Gaia; the Titanomachy), but no Titan is called "ashen." "Ashen Titan" is a composite/game label with no single traditional figure under that exact name. *Sources: Wikipedia, "Titan (mythology)"; Hesiod, Theogony.*

### Ur-Dragon — ADD
"Ur-" is a German prefix meaning proto-/original (Wiktionary). The Ur-Dragon as a named being is Magic: The Gathering lore (Invasion-era flavor text; 2017 Commander set) — no pre-modern folklore figure exists under that name. *Sources: Wiktionary, "ur-"; Wikipedia, "Ur-Dragon."*

## KEEP — 48 (no reliable source for the creature as named)

Grouped by lore part. Each entry: what is real behind it, and why the flag stays.

### Part 1 — oozes (10)
- **Cave Jelly — KEEP.** No story of its own; nearest real cousins are cave bacterial colonies/slime moulds and the folk notion of star jelly (real folklore). A living cave-ceiling jelly is the game's invention. *Doc's entry; star jelly confirmed by Wikipedia, "Star jelly."*
- **Amber Ooze — KEEP.** No amber monster in folklore; amber itself is the tradition (golden, treacherous to anything small that lands in it while soft). *Doc's entry.*
- **Mire Pudding — KEEP.** Bog folklore is real (bogs preserve and swallow; will-o'-the-wisps dance over them), but no creature called a mire pudding. *Doc's entry; will-o'-the-wisp confirmed in the Lanternjaw search.*
- **Gloom Ooze — KEEP.** No gloom ooze in folklore; the fear behind it (darkness that is not empty) is old, but no creature. *Doc's entry.*
- **Mercury Slime — KEEP.** No living quicksilver creature in myth; mercury's own behaviour (pooling, splitting into droplets, reforming) is the lore. *Doc's entry.*
- **Primordial Ooze — KEEP.** Real science, not folklore and not a creature: Oparin (1924) and Haldane (1929, "hot dilute soup") proposed it; Miller–Urey (1953) made amino acids from sparks. *Sources: TheFreeDictionary, "Primordial soup"; Biology LibreTexts 20.3.*
- **Grotto Snapper — KEEP.** No folklore found; the only hits are Flight Rising dragon skins and a Grootslang video. Snapping turtles are real; no "grotto snapper." Tangential note: the Grootslang (South African San tradition — primordial beast, part elephant/part serpent, in a cave called the Wonder Hole) is a real cave monster, but it is not a snapper. *Search: no folklore source.*
- **Thornhound — KEEP.** No folklore; the name appears only in modern games/fiction — Ben 10 (plant-like alien), Oathsworn, *The Verdant Wound* 5e adventure, webnovels. Closest real tradition is hellhounds (Cerberus, Garm, Black Shuck). *Search: Ben 10 Wiki; DriveThruRPG.*
- **Spore Servant — KEEP.** A real D&D term, not folklore: *Monster Manual* p. 230–231, myconid animating spores raise a corpse as a spore servant; also Baldur's Gate 3 Duergar Spore Servants. *Sources: D&D Beyond; Anyflip Monster Manual p. 231; bg3.wiki.*
- **Lanternjaw — KEEP.** "Lanternjaw" is a real English word (a long thin jaw; also a deep-sea fish with a luminous tongue, per the *Eyewitness* transcript) and there is a real "Lantern Man" (will-o'-the-wisp variant, Wicken Fen, ~1800, collected by folklorist L. F. Newman). No Lanternjaw creature in folklore. *Sources: D&D Beyond homebrew; MythosJourney, "Lantern man"; Dorling Kindersley Vision transcript.*

### Part 2 — beasts (6)
- **Crag Ram — KEEP.** Ram symbolism is real (the Golden Fleece ram; Khnum, the ram-headed Egyptian god who shaped humans on his potter's wheel; Aries), but no crag-ram figure. *Doc's entry.*
- **Deep Stag — KEEP.** Cernunnos is real (Pillar of the Boatmen, 1st c. CE; Gundestrup Cauldron; ~25 depictions) — but "no surviving ancient text explicitly defines his functions… No surviving Gaulish narrative text fills the gap." White stag, Actaeon, and Herne the Hunter are real stag lore, but a stag god is not a stag, and there is no deep-stag creature. *Sources: Wikipedia, "Cernunnos"; World History Encyclopedia; The Hush Hollow (Léonet 2022).*
- **Grotto Bull — KEEP.** Apis is real and deep — sacred bull of Memphis, living manifestation of Ptah, later Osiris-Apis/Serapis; Herodotus's markings (black coat, white forehead mark, scarab under the tongue); Serapeum at Saqqara; cult from the 2nd Dynasty into the Roman period. But "Grotto Bull" is a game label, not a creature. *Sources: BiblicalTraining, "Apis"; New World Encyclopedia; Antiquarienne.*
- **Blind Mole — KEEP.** Real animal; European folk belief (moles as creatures of the dark earth, molehills as omens, mole parts as charms). No blind-mole monster. *Doc's entry.*
- **Storm Wraith — KEEP.** No single named storm wraith; real storm beings exist (Tlaloc of the Mexica; Illapa of the Andes, who made lightning by flashing his garments and thunder by cracking his whip). *Doc's entry.*
- **Cipactli Brood — KEEP.** Cipactli itself is real and deep — Aztec primordial crocodile, part crocodile/part fish/part toad, mouths at every joint; Tezcatlipoca sacrificed his foot as bait, then he and Quetzalcoatl tore it apart to make earth and sky; *Leyenda de los Soles* (Codex Chimalpopoca, c. 1558); Sahagún's *Florentine Codex* Book 7; first day-sign of the tonalpohualli. But "Cipactli Brood" — its offspring — has no tradition. *Sources: Wikipedia, "Cipactli"; SpiritualAnimals.com (primary sources cited).*

### Part 3 — goblin/orc/troll/human set (8)
- **Goblin Sneak — KEEP.** "Goblin Sneak" is a tabletop-game role (Pathfinder *Monster Codex*, Goblin Sneak CR 1, goblin rogue 2; PF2e homebrew; Legend of the Five Rings Goblin Sneaks; a Magic card; GURPS). Goblins themselves are real folklore; the "Sneak" is a game role. *Sources: pfprd.de; paizo.com; l5r.fandom.com.*
- **Goblin Champion — KEEP.** No traditional figure called Goblin Champion; goblin kings/chiefs exist in scattered stories (the Goblin King of *The Princess and the Goblin*), but no named champion or rite. *Doc's entry.*
- **Half-Ogre — KEEP.** No folktale; ogres in folklore don't get families or half-human children — a game category grading brute strength between orc and ogre. *Doc's entry.*
- **Marsh Troll — KEEP.** No creature called Marsh Troll; Scandinavian trolls live in mountains/rocks/forests/caves, and marsh spirits (nøkken, grindylow, Jenny Greenteeth) exist, but no named marsh troll stands between them. *Doc's entry.*
- **Ogre Mage — KEEP.** No traditional folktale; closest ancestor is the Japanese oni as adapted by modern fantasy (the tabletop ogre mage draws on the oni image); no tale gives an ogre a wizard's repertoire. *Doc's entry.*
- **Bandit Chief — KEEP.** No figure called Bandit Chief; outlaw leaders exist (Robin Hood, the Balkan hajduks, highwayman chiefs of ballad) but no single chief with a rite of succession. *Doc's entry.*
- **Mad Alchemist — KEEP.** A literary/game figure, not folkloric; real alchemy is a serious history (Hellenistic Egypt through the Islamic world to early modern Europe), but no dungeon-dwelling phial-thrower. *Doc's entry.*
- **Plague Bearer — KEEP.** No monster called Plague Bearer; a real cluster of plague figures exists (17th-c. plague doctor; plague-spreaders of accusation; personifications like the *Danse Macabre* Death and the biblical Angel of Death). *Doc's entry.*

### Part 4 — undead (5 keep; 2 adds above)
- **Phantom — KEEP.** Greek *phantasma* (φάντασμα) is a real ancient word — apparition/ghost, from *phaino* "to appear"; used by Aeschylus, Euripides, Plato; Matthew 14:26 / Mark 6:49. But it is a generic word, not a creature with stories. *Sources: Strong's Greek 5326; Doroszewska, "Ghosts: The Restless and Unpleasant Dead" (OUP).*
- **Wight Lord — KEEP (borderline — see below).** The draugr/haugbúi tradition is real and deep (*Eyrbyggja saga*, *Grettis saga*, *Laxdæla saga*; *haugbúi* = "mound-dweller"; Kárr inn gamli; "barrow-wight" used in the 1869 Magnússon/Morris translation of *Grettis saga*, before Tolkien). But "Wight Lord" is a rank, not a traditional figure. *Sources: Wikipedia, "Draugr"; norsemyth.org; TheFreeDictionary, "Draugr."*
- **Ashen Seraph — KEEP.** No "Ashen Seraph" in scripture, angelology, or folklore; a modern composite (seraph + fallen/burnt angel). Seraphim themselves are real (Isaiah 6). *Doc's entry; seraphim confirmed by Wikipedia, "Seraphim."*
- **Pit Brute — KEEP.** The Malebranche are real literary demons — Dante's *Inferno*, Cantos 21–23: "Evil Claws," thirteen named demons guarding Bolgia 5, leader Malacoda ("Evil Tail"), grappling hooks, boiling pitch, barrators. But "Pit Brute" as a species name has no separate existence. *Sources: UT Austin DanteWorlds; University of Leeds, Discover Dante; Wikipedia, "Malebranche."*
- **Larva of the Damned — KEEP.** Larvae/lemures are real Roman tradition — the Lemuria festival (May 9, 11, 13), Ovid *Fasti* 5, black beans, "Ghosts of my fathers, depart!"; *larvae* = the malevolent subset of the *lemures*; origin myth: Remus's ghost, Romulus instituting the Remuria. But "of the Damned" is a game modifier. *Sources: Wikipedia, "Lemuria (festival)"; romanmythology.com; ThoughtCo.*

### Part 5 — fey/northern (6)
- **Sea Serpent — KEEP.** Rich diffuse tradition (Tiamat, Leviathan, Jörmungandr, Cetus, Hydra, Scylla; Olaus Magnus 1555; St. Olaf 1028) but no single canonical figure with its own story cycle. *Per the prior audit's findings; no new source contradicts it.*
- **Dark Elf (Dökkálfar) — KEEP.** Attested almost only in *Gylfaginning* 17 and *Hrafnagaldr Óðins*; "blacker than pitch," dwell underground; whether distinct from dwarves is unresolved in scholarship; no stories, heroes, or customs attach by name. *Per the prior audit's findings.*
- **Alfar Raider — KEEP.** The álfar are well attested but vague (*Poetic Edda*, sagas; Völundr "ruler of the elves" in *Völundarkviða*); no saga gives an álfar war-band or raiding party. Raiding is a human/troll activity in Norse sources. *Doc's entry.*
- **Bauchan — KEEP.** One collected tale (J. F. Campbell, *Popular Tales of the West Highlands* — Callum Mor MacIntosh of Lochaber; hobgoblin/brownie, goat shapeshifter, helped and fought his farmer, followed him to America) plus a Katharine Briggs dictionary entry. One tale, no cycle. *Per the prior audit's findings.*
- **Fachan — KEEP.** Single vivid description (Campbell, *Popular Tales of the West Highlands* vol. 4, pp. 297–98 — one eye, one hand from the chest, one leg); Douglas Hyde's Irish parallel; possible Arabic *nesnas/shiqq* influence; no story cycle. *Per the prior audit's findings.*
- **Mora — KEEP.** Slavic, well-documented ethnographically (Vuk Karadžić's dictionary; Friedrich Krauss, *Slavische Volksforschungen* 1908 — keyhole entry, chest pressure, blood-drinking in South Slavic tradition) but no distinct story cycle beyond the shared European mare/mara night-pressure belief. *Per the prior audit's findings.*

### Part 6 — Egyptian/Asian (7 keep; 1 clear above)
- **Guardian Scorpion — KEEP (borderline — see below).** Serket (Serqet/Selkis) is real — Egyptian goddess of protection against scorpion venom; "Isis and the Seven Scorpions" (Serket sends seven named scorpions — Tefen, Befen, Mesetet, Mestetef, Petet, Thetet, Maatet — as Isis's bodyguards); she guards a bend in the river on the route to paradise (*Book of the Two Ways*) and a canopic jar. But "Guardian Scorpion" as a creature is a game label; the tradition is a goddess and her escorts. *Sources: Wikipedia, "Serket"; World History Encyclopedia.*
- **Serpopard — KEEP.** Real art motif — Narmer Palette (c. 3100 BCE), Uruk cylinder seals (3500–3000 BC), serpent-necked lions. But "serpopard" is a modern coinage; "there is no known name for the creature in any ancient texts." No stories attach. *Per the prior audit's findings.*
- **Sha Beast — KEEP.** Real Egyptian totemic animal of Set (slender canid, square ears, forked tail; Naqada III to New Kingdom, ~2,000 years). No independent mythology outside its Set association; identity unresolved. *Per the prior audit's findings.*
- **Cedar Guardian — KEEP (borderline — see below).** Humbaba is very substantial — *Epic of Gilgamesh*, Sumerian Huwawa, guardian of the Cedar Forest appointed by Enlil, the Seven Terrors, killed by Gilgamesh and Enkidu. But the bestiary's "Cedar Guardian" is a generic label, not Humbaba himself — and the game already has apex Humbaba carrying that story. *Per the prior audit's findings.*
- **Mountain Yaoguai — KEEP.** *Yaoguai* (妖怪) is a real broad class — shapeshifting animal/plant/object spirits, *Journey to the West*, *Fengshen Yanyi*, *Legend of the White Snake*. "Mountain Yaoguai" is a descriptive label, not a named figure. *Per the prior audit's findings.*
- **Mogwai — KEEP.** Real Chinese term (Cantonese *mo1 gwai2*, Mandarin *móguǐ*, "demon/evil spirit"); rain-season breeding tradition (Zhao Qiguang 1989, *Asian Folklore Studies*). A general term, not a single figure. *Per the prior audit's findings.*
- **River Long — KEEP.** The *lóng* (龍) is real and deep — Britannica: "a type of majestic beast that dwells in rivers, lakes, and oceans"; Earth Dragon (Dilong) controls the waterways; Dragon Kings (Longwang) deliver rain and protect seafarers; the Four Dragons guard the rivers. But "River Long" is a descriptive label, not a named figure. *Sources: Britannica, "Long"; chinese.mythologyworldwide.com.*

### Part 7 — cosmic (6 keep; 2 adds above)
- **Bramble Imp — KEEP.** Imps are real — Germanic folklore, lesser demons, witch familiars in early modern English witch trials; the Lincoln Imp (14th c.); Old English *impa* = "young shoot/offshoot of a tree." "Bramble" is a habitat variant, not a traditional creature. *Sources: TheFreeDictionary, "Imp"; CiNii Research (Kurokawa, witch-trial imp belief).*
- **Void Horror — KEEP.** No traditional folklore; closest is Lovecraft's Cthulhu Mythos (N'h'll, "the Nothing in All and All in Nothing, The Void"; the Great Old Ones) and modern games (League of Legends Voidborn). "Void Horror" is a generic modern label. *Sources: Wikipedia, "Cthulhu Mythos deities"; Villains Wiki, "Voidborn."*
- **Null Seraph — KEEP.** Seraphim are real (Isaiah 6:1–8, six-winged beings crying "holy, holy, holy"; Hebrew *saraph* = "burning"; Book of Enoch; Revelation 4). "Null" is a modern/computing word; no "Null Seraph" in any tradition. *Sources: Wikipedia, "Seraphim"; 1901 Jewish Encyclopedia.*
- **Entropy Wraith — KEEP.** No folklore; "Entropy Wraiths" appear in a modern children's picture book (*Tippo and Fleck*, Klárová & Končinský) and webfiction. "Wraith" is a real Scots ghost word; "entropy" is a 19th-c. physics term; the pairing is modern. *Sources: westwind-festival.de; Chereads webfiction.*
- **Star Horror — KEEP (borderline — see below).** No creature called "Star Horror"; but a real star-demon tradition exists — the Tzitzimime, Aztec star demons, skeletal beings who descend from the stars to devour humanity when the sun is threatened (e.g. at eclipse). Secondary: Bloch's "star vampire" / Shambler from the Stars (*Weird Tales*, 1935). The name is generic. *Sources: Facebook, "Mexico's Darkest Nightmares" (Tzitzimime); Alchetron, "Star vampire."*
- **The Hollow Choir — KEEP.** No folklore; the phrase appears only in modern fiction/games (Royal Road webfiction, the game *Welcome to Elderfield*, D&D homebrew). Closest real traditions are sirens (song kills) and banshee wails — but no Hollow Choir. *Sources: fables.gg; welcometoelderfield.wiki; royalroad.com.*

## Borderline — 4 keeps the director may clear

These four have a real tradition behind them that maps closely onto the creature, but the name is a generic label or rank rather than a traditional figure. The researcher's call is KEEP in each case; the director can overrule.

- **Cedar Guardian** — Humbaba's story is deep, and "guardian of the cedar forest" *is* Humbaba's defining role, so the role-name maps 1:1 onto a named figure. Against clearing: the bestiary creature is a generic guardian, not Humbaba himself, and apex Humbaba already carries the story.
- **Guardian Scorpion** — Serket's seven named scorpions are a real "guardian scorpions" tradition. Against clearing: the tradition is a goddess and her escorts, not a standalone scorpion creature.
- **Star Horror** — the Tzitzimime are real star demons with a story (descend at eclipse to devour humanity). Against clearing: the tradition has its own proper name; the bestiary creature isn't named Tzitzimitl.
- **Wight Lord** — the draugr/haugbúi tradition is deep, and "barrow-wight" is a real 1869 translation. Against clearing: "Wight Lord" is a rank, not a figure; no "lord of wights" appears in the sagas.
